Abstract
This paper studies simple bilevel problems, where a convex u pper-level function is minimized over
the optimal solutions of a convex lower-level problem. We ﬁr st show the fundamental diﬃculty of simple
bilevel problems, that the approximate optimal value of suc h problems is not obtainable by ﬁrst-order
zero-respecting algorithms. Then we follow recent works to pursue the weak approximate solutions. For
this goal, we propose novel near-optimal methods for smooth and nonsmooth problems by reformulating
them into functionally constrained problems.

call such ˆ xa (ǫf,ǫ g)-absolute optimal solution .

intractable for any zero-respecting ﬁrst-order method to ﬁ nd absolute optimal solutions for Problem (1). Our
negative result shows the fundamental diﬃculty of simple bi level problems compared to classical constrained
optimization problems.

2 Related work
In the literature, various methods [1, 3, 6, 10, 11, 12, 13, 17 , 18, 22, 23, 24, 25, 26] have been proposed to
achieve a (ǫf,ǫ g)-weak optimal solution deﬁned as Equation (3) . Below, we rev iew the existing methods
with non-asymptotic convergence. For ease of presentation , we state the results for ǫf =ǫg =ǫ.
2

Prior results on Lipschitz problems Kaushik and Youseﬁan [13] proposed the averaging iterative ly
regularized gradient (a-IRG) for convex optimization with variational inequality constraints, of which Prob-
lem (1) is a special case. a-IRG achieves the rate of O(1/ǫ4). Shen et al. [24] proposed a method for solving
Problem (1) with O(1/ǫ3) complexity based on the online learning framework. When f is Lipschitz and g
is smooth, Merchav and Sabach [18] proposed a gradient-base d algorithm with O(1/ǫ1/(1−α)) complexity for
anyα ∈ (0.5, 1). However, none of these methods can achieve the optimal rate of O(ǫ−2).
Prior results on smooth problems Samadi et al. [23] proposed the regularized accelerated pro ximal
method (R-APM) with a complexity of O(1/ǫ). Under the additional weak sharp minima condition on g,
the complexity of R-APM improves to O (1/√ǫ). However, this condition is often too strong and does not
hold for many problems, Chen et al. [10] extended the result o f [23] to the more general α-Hölderian error
bound condition. However, their method achieves the optima l rate only when α = 1 , which reduces to the
weak sharp minima condition. Jiang et al. [12] developed a Fr ank-Wolfe type algorithm (CG-BiO) with a
complexity of O (1/ǫ), which approximates X ∗
g by a cutting plane approach. Later on, Cao et al. [6] proposed
an accelerated algorithm with a similar cutting plane appro ach to achieve the rate of O(1/√ǫ) under the weak
sharp minima condition. Recently, Wang et al. [27] reduced P roblem (1) to ﬁnding the smallest c such that
the optimal value of the following parametric problem is g∗: minx∈Rng(x), s.t. f(x) ≤c. They adopted a
bisection method to ﬁnd such a c. To solve this parametric problem, Accelerated Proximal Gr adient method
is applied on g with projection operator onto the sublevel set Fc = {x | f (x) ≤ c}, which we call sublevel
set oracles. This leads to an upper bound of ˜O(1/√ǫ). Such an oracle is obtainable for norm-like functions
such as f (x) = 1
2 ‖x‖2. However, projection onto sublevel sets may be hard for more general functions, such
as MSE loss or logistic loss. Compared with prior works [6, 10 , 23, 27], our proposed methods achieve the
˜O(1/√ǫ) rate under standard assumptions, without relying on the wea k sharp minima condition or sublevel
set oracles.
Comparison with Nesterov’s methods for functionally const rained problems Based on similar
reformulation, Nesterov [19] have proposed algorithms for functionally constrained problems, of which Prob-
lem (4) is a special case: one for smooth problems in Section 2 .3.5, and one for Lipschitz problems in Section
3.3.4. However, the ﬁrst algorithm relies on the strong conv exity of f and ˜g, which does not hold in our
setups. Moreover, both algorithms require quadratic progr am (QP) sub-solvers, which are ineﬃcient in
large-scale problems: the ﬁrst algorithm uses it to iterati vely move t to the root of ψ∗(t), while the second
algorithm uses it to project onto the sublevel set of a piecew ise linear approximation of ψ(t, x). In contrast,
our proposed algorithms have the following advantages: (a) require no strong convexity assumption; (b)
require no QP solvers that lead to additional computational cost; (c) have a uniﬁed framework for both
smooth and Lipschitz problems.
3 Preliminaries
For any x ∈ Rn, let x[j] represent the j-th coordinate of x forj = 1, · · ·,n . The Euclidean ball centered at x
with radius D is denoted as B(x,D ) ≜ {y | ‖y − x‖2 ≤D}. For any closed convex set C ⊆ Rn, the Euclidean
projection of x onto C is denoted by Π C(x) ≜ arg miny∈C ‖y − x‖2. We say a function h is C-Lipschitz in
domain Z if ‖h(x) −h(y)‖2 ≤C‖x − y‖2 for all x, y ∈ Z . We say a diﬀerentiable real-valued function h is
L-smooth if it has L-Lipschitz continuous gradients.
We now state the assumptions on the function class required i n our theoretical results. Complexity
guarantees for ﬁnding an optimal solution to a convex optimi zation problem typically depend on the initial
distance of ‖x∗ − x0‖2, where x0 is a pre-speciﬁed point as the initialization of algorithms . In our setting,
we assume an explicit upper bound D of this value.
Assumption 3.1. Consider Problem (1). We assume that
1. Functions f and g : Rn → R are convex and continuous.
2. For a pre-speciﬁed point x0, the initial distance ‖x∗ − x0‖2 is upper bounded by D, where x∗ is the
optimal solution to Problem (1).

We use Assumption 3.1 throughout this paper, but distinguis h the following two diﬀerent settings.
Assumption 3.2. Consider Problem (1). We assume that f andg areLf -smooth andLg-smooth respectively.
We call such problems (Lf,L g)-smooth problems.
Assumption 3.3. Consider Problem (1). We assume that f and g are Cf -Lipschitz and Cg-Lipschitz in

To study the complexity of solving Problem (1), we make the fo llowing assumption on the algorithms.
Assumption 3.4 (zero-respecting algorithm class) . An iterative method A can access the objective functions
f and g only through a ﬁrst-order black-box oracle, which takes a tes t point ˆx as the input and returns
f (ˆ x),g (ˆ x),∂f (ˆ x),∂g (ˆ x), where ∂f (ˆx),∂g (ˆx) are arbitrary subgradients of the objective functions at ˆx. A

This assumption generalizes the standard deﬁnition of zero-respecting algorithm for single-level mini-
mization problems [7, 19]. Many existing ﬁrst-order method s for Problem (1) fall into this algorithm class,
including [10, 17, 18, 22, 23, 24].
The following concept of ﬁrst-order zero-chain , introduced by Nesterov [19, Section 2.1.2], plays an
essential role in proving lower bounds for zero-respecting algorithms. In our paper, we leverage the chain-
like structure to show the intractability of ﬁnding absolut e optimal solutions.
Deﬁnition 3.1 (ﬁrst-order zero-chain) . We call a diﬀerentiable function h(x) : Rq → R a ﬁrst-order zero-
chain if for any sequence {xk}k≥0 satisfying
xk ∈ Span{∇h(x0), · · ·, ∇h(xk−1)}, k ≥ 1; x0 = 0,
it holds that xk,[j] = 0,k + 1 ≤j ≤q.
Deﬁnition 3.1 deﬁnes diﬀerentiable zero-chain functions. We can similarly deﬁne non-diﬀerentiable zero-
chain, by requiring xk to be in Span{∂h(x0), · · ·,∂h (xk−1)} where ∂h(x) is a (possibly adversarial) subgra-
dient of h at x.

This paper gives a comprehensive study of convex simple bile vel problems. We show that no zero-respecting
ﬁrst-order algorithm can ﬁnd a (ǫf,ǫ g)-absolute optimal solution, justifying the notion of weak o ptimal
solution considered by existing works. We then propose a nov el framework FC-BiO for ﬁnding a (ǫf,ǫ g)-
weak optimal solution. Our proposed methods achieve the nea r-optmal rates of ˜O

remain an open question for future research.

B.2 Warm-start strategy
For the initial point of the subroutine M (SGM in FC-BiO Lip or generalized AGM in FC-BiO Sm ), we exploit
