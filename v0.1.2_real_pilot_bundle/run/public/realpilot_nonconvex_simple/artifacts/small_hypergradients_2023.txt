Abstract
Theoretical properties of bilevel problems are well studie d when the lower-level problem is strongly
convex. In this work, we focus on bilevel optimization probl ems without the strong-convexity assumption.
In these cases, we ﬁrst show that the common local optimality measures such as KKT condition or
regularization can lead to undesired consequences. Then, w e aim to identify the mildest conditions
that make bilevel problems tractable. We identify two class es of growth conditions on the lower-level
objective that leads to continuity. Under these assumption s, we show that the local optimality of the
bilevel problem can be deﬁned via the Goldstein stationarit y condition of the hyper-objective. We then
propose the Inexact Gradient-Free Method (IGFM) to solve th e bilevel problem, using an approximate
zeroth order oracle that is of independent interest. Our non -asymptotic analysis demonstrates that the
proposed method can ﬁnd a (δ, ε ) Goldstein stationary point for bilevel problems with a zero th order
oracle complexity that is polynomial in d, 1/δ and 1/ε .

Q1: What is a good deﬁnition of local optimality for bilevel o ptimization without lower-level strong

Some recent works [53, 67, 75] try to address this problem by r esorting to relaxed versions of (1), such as
adding regularization to the lower-level objective or refo rmulating it as a functional constrained optimization.
We give a thorough discussion of these approaches in Section 3, and demonstrate with counterexamples

We further prove in Theorem 4.1 that the continuity of ϕ(x) can be fully characterized by the continuity

discontinuity of S(x), which may happen even if the lower-level objective is linea r and hence arbitrarily
smooth (See Example 3.1). Therefore, the smoothness of lowe r-level objectives does not guarantee an easy
deﬁnition for local optimality of the bilevel problem, and w e need to study a more structured function class.
This raises the second question:
Q2: When can local optimality be well deﬁned for bilevel prob lems?
To answer this question, we identify two conditions that gua rantee the well-behavedness of ϕ(x). We
demonstrate that if g(x, y) is a Lipschitz objective with weak sharp minimum (Assumptio n 4.2) or a smooth
objective with dominant gradients (Assumption 4.3), ϕ(x) is indeed Lipschitz (Proposition C.3). These
conditions hold for a few real-world applications, and stri ctly relax previous assumptions as they do not
require S(x) to be a singleton.
We then show that these two types of assumptions fall into a ge neral class of growth conditions (Propo-
sition D.3). As shown in Section 4 and Section 5.2, growth con ditions serve as an important requirement for
bilevel optimization and meanwhile preserves the structur e of the original problem, since the solution of the
lower-level objective is still non-singleton. Therefore, these assumptions are much more general than strong
convexity.
For a general Lipschitz, nonsmooth, nonconvex function ϕ(x), it is impossible to deﬁne its local optimality
via standard ﬁrst order stationery points, since ϕ(x) can be non-diﬀerentiable. Inspired by the recent
developments of nonconvex, nonsmooth optimization analys is, we formalize the local optimality of bilevel
problem (1) via the notion of Goldstein stationary point [18 , 76] of the hyper-objective ϕ(x). We show
that Goldstein stationary point is a valid deﬁnition of loca l optimality. Unlike approximate KKT points,
Goldstein stationary points subsume optimal solutions of b ilevel problems (Proposition 4.1 and 4.2).
Based on these investigations, we are now ready to study the a lgorithms for solving (1), namely,
Q3: How to ﬁnd a Goldstein stationary point for bilevel optimi zation without lower-level strong convexity?
We propose a polynomial time algorithm (See Theorem 5.2) to ﬁ nd the Goldstein stationary point of the
bilevel problem in Section 5.1. The proposed method, which i s named the Inexact Gradient-Free Method
(IGFM), extends the recently developed zeroth order approa ches in nonsmooth optimization [46], by using
a possibly biased zeroth order oracle of ϕ(x) to construct gradient estimates, and perform noisy gradien t

Unlike standard optimization problems, obtaining an eﬃcie nt zeroth order oracle for bilevel problem

are still computationally intractable (Proposition 5.1). This again demonstrates the hardness of bilevel
optimization and underscores the necessity of focusing on a more restrictive class of lower-level objectives.
Fortunately, we show the possibility to provide a computati onally eﬃcient zeroth order oracle under the
aforementioned growth conditions, i.e. under Assumption 4.2 or 4.3, in Proposition D.4. Observing t he

3 Local Optimality without Lower-level Strong Convexity
In this section, we discuss the property of the hyper-object ive ϕ(x). We ﬁrst demonstrate that Problem (1)
can be extremely ill-posed when the lower-level problem is c onvex but not strongly convex. Previous works
try to overcome this intractability by studying various rel axations on (1), e.g., adding regularization to the
lower-level objective or allowing inexact minimization of it. However, we demonstrate with counterexamples
that these relaxations will distort the lower minimization structure and therefore fail to yield a locally optimal
solution to the original problem (1).
3.1 The Lack of Strong Convexity Leads to Discontinuity
Many previous works [17, 23, 31, 66, 74] assume that g(x, y) is strongly convex in y. In this case, S(x) is
a single value mapping and ϕ is continuously diﬀerentiable. Following the standard ana lysis in nonconvex
optimization, the goal is to ﬁnd a near stationary point of ϕ(x), i.e., ‖∇ϕ(x)‖ ≤ ε. However, without the
strong convexity, this goal is not suitable since S(x) becomes a set-valued mapping, and ϕ(x) can even be
discontinuous. For instance, consider the following examp le.

4 Continuity and Local Optimality of Hyper-Objective
As we have discussed, in general KKT conditions cannot reﬂec t the local optimality or stationary of ϕ(x).
Therefore we resort to a diﬀerent way to deﬁne the local optim ality of the hyper-objective. To get a better
understanding of the ill behavior of ϕ(x), we prove in Theorem 4.1 that the continuity behavior of ϕ(x) is
intrinsically related to that of S(x) in Hausdorﬀ distance. Based on these observations, we intro duce the
notion of Goldstein stationary point, which, as we show late r, serves as a valid stationary measure for bilevel
optimization.
4.1 Continuity of S(x) and ϕ (x)
As shown in Example 3.1, ϕ can be discontinuous without the strong convexity of the low er-level problem.
Unfortunately, optimizing a discontinuous function is imp ossible in general. In this section, we delve into
the discontinuity issue and study the condition that can gua rantee the continuity of ϕ.
Below we demonstrate that the Lipschitz continuity of the hy per-objective ϕ(x) is closed related to the
Lipschitz continuity of the feasible set mapping S(x), in the sense of Hausdorﬀ distance.

6 Conclusions and Future Directions
In this paper, we investigate the local optimality of bileve l optimization without lower-level strong convexity.
We demonstrate that Goldstein stationary point can charact erize the optimality for a general problem class,
and propose the IGFM Algorithm for ﬁnding a Goldstein statio nary point in polynomial time.
We provide several concluding remarks. Firstly, the comple xity of the proposed method has a possibly
heavy dependency on d and 1/ε. It would be interesting to apply acceleration methods on th e outer gradient-
free method as well as the lower zeroth order oracle evaluati on. Secondly, this paper mainly focuses on the
zeroth order approach in general bilevel optimization. The ﬁrst order approaches, although widely adopted
in previous works, are hard to apply in the absence of lower-l evel strong convexity. The major diﬃculty
lies in the hardness of obtaining an eﬃcient ﬁrst order oracl e, due to the nested structure of (1) and the
possible nondiﬀerentiability of the hyper-objective. As s hown in section 5.2, it is highly nontrivial even to
obtain a zeroth order oracle. It would be a worthwhile direct ion to propose an eﬃcient ﬁrst order algorithm
for ﬁnding Goldstein stationary points in bilevel problems , or establishing corresponding hardness results.
Thirdly, we remark that the IGFM framework is also suitable f or general non-convex objectives, as long as
an eﬃcient zeroth order oracle exists. Therefore, it is wort h considering relaxing the convexity assumption
of the lower-level objective. Other future directions incl ude more general settings such as stochastic case
and variational inequalities problems with variational in equality constraints.
