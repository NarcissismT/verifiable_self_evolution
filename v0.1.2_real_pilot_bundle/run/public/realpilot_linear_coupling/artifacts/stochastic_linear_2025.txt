Abstract
In this work, we develop analysis and algorithms for a class of (stochastic) bilevel op-
timization problems whose lower-level (LL) problem is strongly convex and linearly con-
strained. Most existing approaches for solving such problems rely on unrealistic assumptions
or penalty function-based approximate reformulations that are not necessarily equivalent
to the original problem. In this work, we develop a stochastic algorithm based on an im-
plicit gradient approach, suitable for data-intensive applications. It is well-known that for
the class of problems of interest, the implicit function is nonsmooth. To circumvent this
difficulty, we apply a smoothing technique that involves adding small random (linear) per-
turbations to the LL objective and then taking the expectation of the implicit objective over
these perturbations. This approach gives rise to a novel stochastic formulation that ensures
the differentiability of the implicit function and leads to the design of a novel and efficient
doubly stochastic algorithm. We show that the proposed algorithm converges to an ( ϵ,δ)-
Goldstein stationary point of the stochastic objective in eO(ϵ−4δ
−1
) iterations. Moreover,
under certain additional assumptions, we establish the same convergence guarantee for the
algorithm to achieve a (3 ϵ,δ +O(ϵ))-Goldstein stationary point of the original objective.
Finally, we perform experiments on adversarial training (AT) tasks to showcase the utility
of the proposed algorithm.

Computing this gradient requires access to the (exact) LL optimal solution y∗(x) and the dif-
ferentiability of the map y∗(x) : Rdu→ Rdℓ. The former requirement can typically be relaxed
to having an approximate solution by(x)≈ y∗(x), which leads to an approximated implicit gra-
dient [15]. However, the latter requirement is often not easy to satisfy, as the solution mapping
y∗(x) may not be differentiable for every x∈ Rdu.
It is known that when the LL problem is strongly convex and unconstrained, then by the
Implicit Function Theorem [15] we can derive an explicit expression for ∇y∗(x) for every x∈
Rdu. For this reason, the majority of recent works have focused on developing algorithms for
unconstrained bilevel problems [15–19]. However, when the LL problem is constrained, y∗(x)
might not be differentiable.
A few works have tackled the bilevel problem with specific types of LL constraints [20–28].
For example, the work in [25] solves bilevel problems with linear equality constraints, the
works in [24, 26, 27] address bilevel problems with linear inequality constraints, and the works
[20–23,28] tackle bilevel problems with general constraints in the LL problem. For linear equality-
constrained problems, differentiability of the map y∗(x) can be established under standard
assumptions [25, Lemma 2]; however, for linear inequality constrained problems, it may be non-
differentiable. The work [27] imposes restrictive assumptions (such as strict complementarity)
that guarantee differentiability of y∗(x) while the authors in [24] establish almost sure differ-
entiability of y∗(x) by introducing a small linear perturbation in the LL problem. In contrast
to [24, 27], the authors in [26] do not rely on ensuring differentiability of y∗(x), they instead
develop first-order (Hessian free) algorithms building upon the theory of non-smooth optimiza-
tion [29–31]. All these works aim to develop implicit gradient methods with finite-time (or

Contributions of this work. In this work, we study the stochastic bilevel problem (1),
where the LL task has a strongly convex objective and linear constraints that couple both the
UL and the LL variables. This is a challenging problem, and due to its stochasticity and non-
smoothness of the implicit function, so far we are not aware of any work that has developed sat-
isfactory algorithms for it. Comparing to the guarantees developed in the above-referred works,
we design algorithms with the following characteristics: ① Dimension-independent and non-
asymptotic convergence guarantees unlike [26] and [20], respectively; ② Stationarity measures
defined directly based on both the perturbed and the original implicit functions, unlike [21–23];
③ No restrictive assumptions such as strict complementarity [27], weak convexity on the implicit
objective [24] or having access and boundedness of the dual parameters to guarantee conver-
gence [26], and ④ Stochastic objective function in contrast to majority of works discussed above
that only work under the deterministic setting [20–24, 26–28]. Specifically, the contributions of
this work are:
• We propose a random perturbation-based smoothing technique that makes the implicit
objective F (x) almost surely differentiable. Then, taking the expectation of F (x) over these
perturbations leads to a novel stochastic formulation that guarantees the differentiability of
the (stochastic) implicit function. Additionally, a closed-form expression is obtained for the
stochastic implicit gradient that facilitates the development of gradient-based algorithms.
• The stochastic problem is challenging since the smoothed stochastic implicit objective does
not have Lipschitz continuous gradients. As a result, conventional gradient-based algorithms
may fail. To address this, we propose DS-BLO, a doubly stochastic method for solving linearly
constrained stochastic bilevel optimization problems that utilizes two perturbations, one for
tackling non-differentiability and the other for non-Lipschitz smoothness of the implicit objective.
We establish finite-time convergence guarantees provided by DS-BLO and show that it converges
to an (ϵ,δ)-stationary solution of the perturbed problem in eO(ϵ−4δ
−1
) iterations. Importantly,
under an additional set of assumptions we establish the same convergence guarantee to achieve
an (3ϵ,bδ)-Goldstein stationary solution of the original problem (1) with bδ = δ +ϵ/eLFq where
eLFq is defined in Lemma A.5. To our knowledge, this is the first algorithm that guarantees
(finite-time) convergence for stochastic bilevel problems with LL constraints without directly
imposing any assumptions on the implicit function.
• Finally, we assess the effectiveness of the proposed method through experiments on an
adversarial learning problem. Our experiments show that DS-BLO demonstrates improved
performance against existing bilevel approaches while providing competitive performance against
popular adversarial learning baselines even though DS-BLO is not customized for adversarial
learning problems.
Comparison with the conference version [24]. This work significantly extends and im-
proves upon the formulation and algorithms presented in its conference version [24], as explained
below. ① In [24], a perturbed deterministic problem is tackled, whereas this work proposes a
more general stochastic formulation where the expectation over the random perturbations (and
stochastic samples) is computed, making the objective function differentiable. ② The formu-
lation in [24] utilized a single perturbation throughout the execution of the algorithm which
required restrictive assumptions on the problem such as (weak) convexity to develop algorithms
with finite-time convergence guarantees. In contrast, this work treats the perturbation as sam-
pled from certain distributions, allowing us to develop algorithms with finite-time convergence
guarantees without imposing restrictive assumptions on the implicit function class. ③ In [24],
without restrictive assumptions, only asymptotic convergence is provided by directly leveraging

2.1 The non-smoothness of problem (1)
It can be easily shown that under Assumptions 1 and 2, the LL solution map y∗(x), and thus
the implicit function F (x) of problem (1) are both continuous (see [24, Proposition 2.2]), but
not differentiable in general [24, Section 2.1]. This implies that the implicit function, F (x),
for problem (1) is generally non-smooth. Therefore, the standard notion of stationarity (cf.
Definition 2.1) is not suitable for problem (1). In the following, we introduce the notions
of stationarity utilized in (non)smooth optimization literature and existing efforts that design
algorithms to compute them in the context of bilevel optimization.

For minimizing general non-smooth Lipschitz functions, initial attempts to develop (sub)
gradient-based methods to obtain (ϵ,δ)-Goldstein stationary points relied on gradient sampling
methods [38], which suffered from dimension dependence, i.e., the developed convergence guar-
antees relied on the problem dimension. Recently, a series of works, including [29–31], have devel-
oped perturbation-based algorithms for minimizing single-level non-smooth Lipschitz functions
and established finite-time dimension-free guarantees to achieve an ( ϵ,δ)-Goldstein stationary
point. Very recently, the authors in [39] considered the problem of minimizing non-smooth Lips-
chitz functions and established that this additional perturbation is necessary to obtain finite-time
dimension-free convergence guarantees to reach an (ϵ,δ)-Goldstein stationary point.
First-order methods for solving linearly constrained bilevel problems of the form (1) have
recently been developed in [26], building upon ideas from [29–31]. However, since [26] utilizes
the first-order gradients to estimate the Hessian (inverses), the derived convergence guarantees
either depend on the problem dimension or assume precise access and boundedness of dual
parameters of the LL problem to achieve an ( ϵ,δ)-Goldstein stationary point. We note that
ensuring the boundedness of the optimal dual variables and the access to them is not feasible
in practice. Please see Table 2 for a comparison. To our knowledge, developing algorithms with
dimension-free finite-time guarantees for solving problem (1) is still an open problem.
The above discussion serves as a motivation to develop algorithms for solving bilevel problems
of the form (1) that guarantee dimension-independent finite-time convergence guarantees and
does not impose restrictive assumptions on the implicit function or the dual variables. Naturally,
this leads to the development of a“doubly” perturbation-based algorithm where one perturbation
is utilized for ensuring dimension-independent guarantees [39] while the second perturbation (cf.
formulation (2)) is used to guarantee differentiablity of the implicit objective circumventing the
need for restrictive assumptions on the objective or the dual variables.

obtaining an (approximate) solution to the lower-level problem, and② evaluating the expressions

3 A doubly stochastic algorithm for linearly constrained bilevel
optimization (DS-BLO)
In this section, we develop an algorithm to solve the stochastically perturbed problem (2). Note
that even though perturbed problem (2) is now differentiable, designing an efficient algorithm
for it is still challenging since the implicit function F (·) is not Lipschitz smooth. This can
be observed from Proposition 2.3 which shows that ∇y∗
q(x) is not a continuous function of x
because the active set A is discontinuous in x. However, we know from Lemma 2.4 that F (·) is
a Lipschitz continuous function. This implies that conventional gradient-based algorithms are
no longer suitable for (2).
To proceed, we develop a novel doubly stochastic algorithm DS-BLO, which optimizes the
perturbed problem (2). DS-BLO relies on two kinds of perturbations, one is the vector q∼Q
introduced in Section 2 to ensure the differentiability of the stochastic objective. The second
perturbation is necessary to achieve dimension-free convergence guarantees as recently estab-
lished in [39]. In contrast to [26] where the authors directly tackle the non-smooth problem (1),
the perturbed reformulation in (2) is differentiable. Even though the function is differentiable,
the function may still be non-Lipschitz smooth, therefore, we rely on the standard notion of
(ϵ,δ)-Goldstein stationarity to provide the convergence guarantees.

Assumption 6. We assume that the following hold

5 Conclusion
In this work, we developed a framework for solving a special class of constrained bilevel problems
where the LL task has linear constraints and a strongly convex objective. The key challenge
we tackled for solving this class of problems is the non-differentiability of the implicit function,
which is addressed with the use of a perturbation-based smoothing technique. This allows us to
compute the gradient of the implicit function in closed form, and develop first-order algorithms
to compute its stationary points in finite time. In the future, we would be interested in studying
other special classes of constrained bilevel problems (i.e., problems with different constraint sets)
and their properties.
